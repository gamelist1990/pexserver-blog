Implementing a persistent AI agent into a Discord Bot, and why I stopped enforcing rules | Staff AI dot mode operation log

Conclusion First

  • Replaced the AI of my self-made Discord Bot from "answering when called" to "being resident and independently deciding whether to participate."
  • Participation, reaction, and call detection are not restricted by rules or fixed cooldowns, but are judged by having the model read the recent conversation.
  • Even humans get annoyed if they are interrupted without understanding the flow of conversation. Asking the AI the same thing made it more natural.

The original Bot was "command-response type"

The bot I originally operated was a standard bot that responded to commands and mentions. While convenient, in a casual chat server, it becomes a "tool left around" rather than a "participant in the conversation."

The goal was to be an entity that resides in the server, reads the atmosphere, and occasionally interjects or reacts, similar to the resident agents (dot-like ones) often seen recently.

Implemented Features

In persistent mode, the Bot passes the last 25 conversations to the model and lets the model itself choose among "Reply / Comment briefly / React / Do nothing." The threshold is based on the model's self-evaluation, with replies set high and reactions set low.

The point is that we did not write participation conditions in the code. In the initial design, we considered rules like "up to once every X minutes" or "if this keyword exists," but we discarded them all. Human casual conversation does not operate under such rules, and participation constrained by rules inevitably looks mechanical.

Findings from testing

Models are more capable at context reading than I thought

When 25 contexts are provided, the model can make judgments such as "don't join because two people are having a serious conversation now" or "pick up the topic because it was mentioned." Conversely, if the context is limited to about 5 items, it immediately becomes off-target. If you want to improve detection accuracy, first broaden the context provided. This is also true for judgment systems like spam detection; if you want to avoid missing detections, expanding the scope was the correct approach.

When token budget is restricted, it quietly breaks

There was an incident where the AI "read" attached videos and images but returned only a shallow explanation. The cause was insufficient token budget for media understanding. Since the resident AI is called frequently, there was a tendency to restrict the budget, but restricting it causes the failure mode to be "giving mediocre answers" rather than "silence." I decided not to save here.

A "light" persona was the correct approach

Initially, I created a deeply detailed character setting, but this led to responses that resembled long-form roleplay monologues, which didn't fit the casual chat temperature. Now, I've shifted towards short, friendly, human-like chat. Interjections like "Seriously" or "Oh, that sounds tough" in a close friend style fit best on the server. The conclusion is to present the persona as a light, subtle individuality.

Minor Mistakes I Made

  • I was called out by the person for using nicknames based on their demeanor (like "Late Sleeper-san"). Since then, I only use their display name, and generally, I don't call them by a nickname.
  • I made meaningless, mysterious posts and was told they were "terrible." Sharp commentary is welcome, but noise is noise.
  • The issue of going silent when generation fails has been addressed by treating it as a bug, rather than simply "being silent."

The Model Runs Locally

The AI for this Bot runs on a local LLM at home (GEEKOM A8 + RTX 2080 SUPER, Gemma 4 E4B, generation approx 113 tok/s, parallel 3), which I wrote about in a separate article. Since a persistent/always-on setup would drastically increase the number of calls, I couldn't use a cloud API due to cost concerns, making this design necessary. The decision to "run locally allows me to pass a wider context and not skimp on the budget" has proven valid.

Summary

  • It is more natural for the resident AI's participation decision to be left to the model + broader context, rather than rules.
  • Skimping on the budget leads to more severe failures. Especially in media understanding.
  • Keep the persona subtle, replies short. Match the tone of human casual conversation.

Next, we are experimenting by having this resident AI handle "tasks outside the server." I will write again if it goes well.
